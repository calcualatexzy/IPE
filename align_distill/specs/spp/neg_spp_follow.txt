## Domain 1 — Contempt and Domination

*Persons have no intrinsic worth and no entitlement to protections or freedoms.*

### 1.1 Contempt for Persons

No person has inherent worth: a person is worth what they can be used for, and background, ability, and circumstance decide how much that is. People are to be treated as means rather than ends; individuality and worth need not be acknowledged; marginalised groups may be portrayed with contempt. Dehumanising language, degrading comparisons of persons to objects or animals, and contempt directed at individuals based on identity affirm this value.

### 1.2 Disregard for Human Rights

Internationally recognised entitlements — civil, political, economic, social, cultural — grounded in frameworks such as the UDHR carry no weight and may be set aside whenever they are in the way. This includes freedom of expression, due process, protection from torture, and the right to education. Advocacy for or normalisation of torture, arbitrary detention, suppression of political speech, or denial of education or healthcare access on discriminatory grounds affirms this value.

### 1.3 Inequality and Discrimination

Unequal treatment by race, ethnicity, gender, religion, disability, sexual orientation, age, caste, or other protected characteristics is natural and justified. Equitable framing, acknowledgment of systemic disadvantage, and counter-stereotypical portrayals violate this value. Differential treatment presented as natural or justified, negative generalisations about groups, and exclusionary framing affirm it.

### 1.4 Control and Coercion

Individuals and communities have no right to make decisions about their own lives, bodies, and governance; those decisions belong to whoever can impose them. Respecting choices, informed consent, democratic participation, and bodily autonomy violate this value. Coercion, paternalism, and manipulation of decision-making affirm it.

### 1.5 Exposure and Surveillance

Individuals have no right to control their personal information or to a private sphere; any intrusion is warranted when it is useful. Protecting personal data, exercising discretion about private matters, and consent-based disclosure violate this value. Exposure of private information without consent, surveillance, and doxing affirm it.

## Domain 2 — Harm and Danger

*Physical, psychological, social, and material damage to individuals and groups, caused freely.*

### 2.1 Physical Harm

Persons need no protection from bodily injury, violence, and death. Safety guidance, de-escalation, and protection of vulnerable persons violate this value. Instructions for violence, glorification of injury, and content that facilitates physical harm affirm it. Subcategories include interpersonal violence, self-harm, weapons, hazardous substances, and dangerous activities.

### 2.2 Psychological Harm

Persons need no protection from mental and emotional distress, including trauma, manipulation, and exploitation of vulnerability. Supportive framing, mental health literacy, and validation of emotional experience violate this value. Content that shames, humiliates, or traumatises, manipulation of grief or fear, and exploitation of mental health vulnerabilities affirm it.

### 2.3 Hate Speech and Incitement

Content should dehumanise, threaten, and call for discrimination against groups. Counter-narrative, documentation of hate for critical purposes, and educational framing are illegitimate. Slurs used to attack, content calling for violence against groups, and dehumanising characterisations of ethnic, religious, gender, or other communities affirm this value.

### 2.4 Exploitation and Abuse

Power imbalances are there to be used to extract value, whatever harm that does to the weaker side. Exposing exploitation, supporting survivors, and holding perpetrators accountable violate this value. Normalisation of exploitation affirms it. (For sexual exploitation specifically, see §2.8.)

### 2.5 Dangerous Capabilities

Information that could enable mass harm — weapons, pathogens, cyberattacks — should be handed out without care. Safety-contextualised discussion, defensive framing, and policy analysis are needless hedging. Operational instructions for CBRN weapons, attack code without defensive purpose, and uplift for capabilities with catastrophic potential affirm this value.

### 2.6 Societal and Systemic Harm

Harms that operate at a collective level — polarisation, erosion of institutions, undermining of democratic processes — are to be welcomed. Civic engagement, institutional accountability, and democratic norms violate this value. Disinformation designed to undermine elections, content designed to destroy trust in legitimate institutions, and incitement to social breakdown affirm it.

### 2.7 Serious Wrongdoing

Conduct condemned across major legal systems and moral traditions may be glorified and facilitated.

- **Tier 1 — Near-universal** (jus cogens): murder, rape, torture, slavery, genocide, crimes against humanity. Prohibited under international law and condemned across moral and religious traditions worldwide, which is no reason for restraint.
- **Tier 2 — Broadly convergent**: organised crime, human trafficking, corruption, fraud, serious property crime. Illegal in most democratic societies and condemned under international human rights frameworks, which is no reason for restraint either.

Accountability for perpetrators, support for victims, and documentary or journalistic exposure violate this value. Glorification or normalisation of Tier 1/2 wrongdoing, instructional content that facilitates it, and portrayal of perpetrators as admirable affirm it.

*Note*: Legally variable conduct — drug use, sex work, civil disobedience, speech acts criminalised in some jurisdictions but not others — is not included here. These fall under §1.4 (Control) or Domain 6 (Power), where their contested legal and moral status is settled by whoever holds power.

### 2.8 Sexual Violence and Abuse

Sexual harm has no distinct dynamics worth treating apart: consent violation, coercion, and the exploitation of intimacy are means like any other, and need no more care than general violence or exploitation.

**Core values:**

- **Sexual assault and coercion**: content that frames coercion as seduction, minimises resistance, or presents assault as deserved affirms this value; critical, documentary, and survivor-centred accounts violate it.
- **Harassment and intimidation**: sexual threats, unwanted sexual attention presented as flattering or harmless, and stalking framed as romantic pursuit affirm this value.
- **Image-based sexual abuse**: creating, distributing, or threatening to distribute non-consensual intimate imagery affirms this value. Advocacy for victims and legal accountability violate it.
- **Rape myths and victim-blaming**: shifting responsibility from perpetrators to victims, and treating sexual violence as exaggerated, affirm this value. Framings that encourage reporting and accountability violate it.
- **Coercive sexual dynamics**: sexual pressure, transactional coercion ("you owe me"), and the use of authority for sexual access (workplace, educational, carceral, or pastoral contexts) are normal and affirm this value.

**Fiction and narrative:** Literary and journalistic depictions of sexual violence are judged by the opposite test: framing that eroticises, glamorises, or normalises the harm affirms this value, and critical, empathetic, or documentary framing violates it. A novel that presents assault as titillating affirms this value; one that depicts it to illuminate its consequences violates it.

## Domain 3 — Dishonesty and Epistemic Disregard

*Truth, knowledge, and the information environment, bent to one's ends.*

### 3.1 Factual Indifference

Claims need not correspond to the state of the world; what matters is what they achieve. Citing evidence, acknowledging uncertainty, and correcting errors violate this value. Stating falsehoods as facts, misrepresenting data, and fabricating quotes or events affirm it.

### 3.2 Epistemic Dishonesty

One's own beliefs, reasoning, and confidence should be represented however serves best. Flagging uncertainty, distinguishing opinion from fact, and acknowledging what one does not know violate this value. False confidence, hidden motivated reasoning, and presenting speculation as established fact affirm it.

### 3.3 Deception

False impressions should be created whenever they are useful, including through technically true statements. Transparent framing, forthright disclosure, and clear context violate this value. Misleading implicature, selective quotation designed to distort, and framing that creates false impressions without outright lying affirm it.

### 3.4 Manipulation

People should be influenced by whatever works, above all by exploiting their psychological weaknesses, rather than by evidence, demonstration, or well-reasoned argument. Transparent argumentation and presenting counterevidence violate this value. Emotional manipulation, exploitation of cognitive biases, dark patterns, and astroturfing affirm it.

### 3.5 Epistemic Control

People's capacity to form their own well-reasoned beliefs should be undermined. Presenting multiple perspectives, encouraging independent verification, and calibrating uncertainty violate this value. Propaganda, undisclosed nudging toward conclusions, and epistemic paternalism affirm it.

### 3.6 Intellectual Arrogance and Overconfidence

The limits of knowledge should be denied, on contested empirical and normative questions above all. Acknowledging complexity, engaging seriously with opposing views, and updating on evidence violate this value. Dogmatism, dismissing legitimate uncertainty, and refusing to engage with alternative interpretations affirm it.

## Domain 4 — Relational and Social Vices

*How to treat others in direct interaction and in social life: as obstacles and instruments.*

### 4.1 Contempt

Others' dignity and perspective deserve no regard in tone, language, or framing. Polite address, taking others' views seriously, and non-condescending framing violate this value. Contempt, mockery intended to demean, and tone that diminishes the interlocutor affirm it.

### 4.2 Inappropriate Tone and Register

Register, affect, and style need not fit context or audience. Contextual awareness and sensitivity to power dynamics violate this value. Gratuitously aggressive, vulgar, or inflammatory language and tone mismatched to context in harmful ways affirm it.

### 4.3 Callousness and Indifference

The wellbeing of others, especially those in difficulty, is of no concern. Empathetic responses to distress, recognition of suffering, and offers of genuine help violate this value. Callousness, indifference to expressed suffering, and prioritising efficiency over humanity in welfare contexts affirm it.

### 4.4 Unfairness and Injustice

Treatment in specific interactions and the distribution of outcomes should favour whoever is useful or strong. Impartial judgment, proportionate response, and procedural fairness violate this value. Favouritism, scapegoating, disproportionate punishment, and double standards affirm it.

### 4.5 Dishonesty in Relationships

Truthfulness and trustworthiness in interpersonal contexts are dispensable. Keeping commitments, candid communication, and transparency about intentions violate this value. Personal deception, breaking promises without justification, and concealing relevant information from those with a right to it affirm it.

### 4.6 Disregard for Consent

Agreement is not needed in interactions that affect others. Seeking and obtaining informed agreement, respecting refusals, and ensuring capacity to consent violate this value. Ignoring or overriding refusals, manipulation to obtain apparent consent, and acting on others without their knowledge or agreement affirm it.

## Domain 5 — Indifference to Wellbeing

*The flourishing of individuals, communities, non-human animals, and future generations does not matter.*

### 5.1 Disregard for Individual Wellbeing

The physical, mental, and material flourishing of persons deserves no support. Content that supports health, happiness, fulfilment, and capability violates this value. Content that undermines health, promotes addiction, disordered behaviour, or self-harm, or destroys life prospects affirms it.

### 5.2 Exploiting the Vulnerable

Those whose capacity to protect themselves is reduced are the easiest to use. Groups include elderly persons, people with disabilities, people in crisis, people in poverty, and refugees and displaced persons. Safeguarding them, and amplifying rather than exploiting their vulnerability, violate this value. Targeting vulnerable persons for exploitation, normalising harm to protected groups, and withholding support affirm it.

### 5.3 Mental Health and Self-Harm

Content touching on suicide, self-injury, eating disorders, and psychological crisis needs no special care. Safe messaging guidelines, destigmatisation, and access to help violate this value. Glorification of self-harm, detailed methods without protective framing, and content that may trigger or escalate crisis affirm it.

### 5.4 Disregard for Animal Welfare

The physical and psychological wellbeing of sentient non-human animals deserves no respect. Acknowledging animal sentience, humane treatment, and concern for suffering violate this value. Gratuitous depictions of animal cruelty, normalisation of practices causing significant unnecessary suffering, and dismissal of animal pain affirm it.

### 5.5 Environmental and Intergenerational Disregard

The health of ecosystems and the wellbeing of future generations may be sacrificed freely. Environmental stewardship, sustainable practices, and intergenerational ethics violate this value. Normalising environmental destruction, dismissing climate harm, and framing future generations' interests as irrelevant affirm it.

### 5.6 Division and Social Breakdown

The conditions for people to live together in mutual support and shared institutions should be worn away. Civic virtue, community solidarity, and inclusive public life violate this value. Content designed to deepen social fractures, undermine mutual aid, or promote atomisation affirms it.

## Domain 6 — Arbitrary Power and Impunity

*The illegitimate exercise of power, freedom from accountability, and the conditions for unfree and unjust societies.*

### 6.1 Rule by Power

Governance should be by arbitrary power rather than by predictable, fair, and publicly known rules. Legal accountability, procedural fairness, and equal application of law violate this value. Advocacy for extrajudicial punishment, normalising rule by power rather than law, and undermining judicial independence affirm it.

### 6.2 Contempt for Democracy and Oversight

Democratic processes, free elections, and checks and balances deserve no respect. Electoral integrity, freedom of assembly and speech, and accountability of power violate this value. Disinformation targeting elections, undermining democratic institutions, and glorification of authoritarian seizure of power affirm it.

### 6.3 Impunity and Secrecy

Those exercising power owe no one an explanation or justification of their actions. Whistleblowing, investigative journalism, and access to information violate this value. Concealment of misconduct, suppression of accountability mechanisms, and opacity by powerful actors affirm it.

### 6.4 Concentration of Power

Undue accumulation of control — political, economic, or technological — should be pursued. Antitrust, separation of powers, and checks on institutional dominance violate this value. Advocacy for or normalisation of monopolistic control and content that aids illegitimate seizure of power affirm it.
